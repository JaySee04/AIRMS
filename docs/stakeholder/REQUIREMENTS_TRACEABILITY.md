**The one thing to walk into viva knowing:** Dr Thung's most elaborated single
request was an administrator view of **injury cases** — how many, which body
part, left or right, what type, trending over time (12:01–12:39). AIRMS no

## 1. Scope: all sports, all programmes ✅

> *"We oversee all sports. We have 52 sports. And in two or three programs …
> national elite program and national development program"* — 08:55–09:27

`lib/sports.ts` carries the 52-sport list; `Athlete.program` is
`PODIUM | PELAPIS | OTHERS`. Cohort norms key on (sport, programme, gender,
discipline), so the multi-programme structure is not just stored, it is the
unit of comparison.

## 2. Whole body, not one sport's parts ✅

> *"instead of focusing only on body parts that are crucial to swimming
> athletes … expand the scope to being basically the whole human body for
> across all 52 sports"* — 09:33

The body map covers the full figure: 22 individually named muscles in flags
mode, five regions in ROM & Stability mode.

## 3. Slice by sport, gender, age group ✅

> *"would you do like dashboard … by sport by gender by age group kind of thing
> to see their overall score and their most issues"* — 11:00
> *"we have the slicer to pick what we want"* — 12:17

`/admin/dashboard` filters on sport / gender / programme / age group, and every
panel follows the filter. The holistic PDF prints **Risk Bands by Sport**,
**by Gender** and **by Age Group** as standing sections.

## 4. Injury summaries for the administrator ⚠️ **RECAST — read this one**

> *"We have 1,000 cases of injury. And what kind of injury, like summaries of
> injuries, like they have upper body, lower body, which part, left or right,
> what kind of type of the injury."* — 12:01–12:17

**This is not in the system.** AIRMS had it — an injury log, body-part and
injury-type distributions, a filtered injury PDF — and **deleted it on
2026-08-02** under the HoloMotion-only directive.

**What stands in its place** is the same *anatomical* question answered from
screening rather than from incident reports:

| Dr Thung asked for | What AIRMS shows now |
|---|---|
| Which body part | Seven per-region risk indicators, banded Low / Watch / Elevated |
| Left or right | Muscle flags carry a side (L / R / Both); symmetry is scored per region |
| Upper / lower body | The five subitem regions (neck, shoulder & upper limbs, torso, pelvis, lower limbs) |
| What type of injury | ✗ **no equivalent** — screening measures risk, not diagnosis |
| How many cases | ✗ **no equivalent** — counts athletes at risk, not injuries sustained |

**The defence, stated plainly:** the injury log was manually maintained and
therefore only as complete as staff remembered to make it, whereas the
HoloMotion report is generated by the instrument for every athlete screened.
AIRMS chose the input ISN actually produces. It also shifts the system from
*recording what already happened* to *flagging who is heading for it*, which is
what "injury risk management" names.

**The cost, stated equally plainly:** ISN can no longer produce the
management-level injury summary Dr Thung described, and AIRMS cannot answer
"how many knee injuries did we have last year". That is a real capability the
stakeholder asked for by name.

**Before viva:** this needs to be Dr Thung's call, not an assumption. Either he
accepts the substitution, or the injury-case summary comes back as a thin
reporting layer. Tracked in [`../fyp/JC_CHECKLIST.md`](../fyp/JC_CHECKLIST.md).

## 5. Trend over time — year to year, quarter to quarter ✅

> *"you can have a graph to show how this progress from time to time, like from
> year to year, quarter to quarter, and see whether it's actually drop"* — 12:39

Built 2026-08-06. `GET /athletes/analytics/periods` buckets the screening
history yearly / quarterly / monthly; `/admin/dashboard` carries the toggle and
the holistic PDF opens with the same table. Direction of travel is shown
against the previous period, and a second reading pairs each athlete with
themselves between successive tests.

## 6. Seasonality — *which* period is the risky one ❌ **GAP**

> *"when is the most critical time, like most of the fourth quarter or
> September, you have high risk of knee injury. The other quarters have very
> low. So then it means we have to do some actions before."* — 12:39–12:54

The period view is **chronological** (Q2 2026, then Q3 2026). It does not fold
Q4-across-all-years together to expose a recurring seasonal peak, which is the
question Dr Thung actually posed — he wants to act *before* the bad quarter
arrives.

Not built, and honestly not yet buildable: it needs multiple years of history
and AIRMS has roughly four months of seeded data. Worth naming as future work
with the reason, rather than leaving it looking overlooked. The aggregation is
small — group by quarter-of-year instead of by (year, quarter) — so it is a
data-maturity gap, not an architectural one.

## 7. Data drives an action plan ⚠️ partial

> *"make a policy, okay, why women actually have more knee. So we need to do
> more knee prevention"* … *"That has helped us to get the action plan done"* —
> 12:17–12:54

The *diagnosis* half works: filter to Female, read the knee indicator's band
distribution, compare against Male. The *prescription* half exists only at the
individual and squad level — the athlete's Training Focus and the coach's
Suggested Focus. There is no institution-level "policy recommendation" output.
Reasonable to leave to the sports scientist, but say so rather than imply the
system closes the loop.

## 8. Upload updates the database in a simple step ✅

> *"if we have done the screening … we can update the database … We just upload
> the data and then it will add into the picture and then people will just,
> without any, you know, it's simple step"* — 13:00

Drag HoloMotion PDFs onto `/medical/data-upload` or `/admin/data-upload`; they
extract automatically, the operator confirms a preview, and the commit
re-scores the cohort and emails the flagged athletes' staff. Batch-capable.

## 9. The doctor traces an athlete by IC number ✅

> *"the medical doctor in the room … they can trace through their IC number.
> Let's say the IC number to just to see, oh, this athlete have this problem"*
> — 13:00

`/medical/dashboard`'s roster searches name **or** IC, and every row shows the
IC beneath the name. Verified live: `?search=890202` returns the matching
athlete.

**This quote is also the justification for making the IC the athlete key**
(A2, 2026-08-04) — a decision flagged elsewhere as a privacy trade-off needing
a viva answer. The answer is here, in the stakeholder's own words: the IC is
how ISN's clinicians identify an athlete, so it is the identifier the system
has to reconcile on. Cite 13:00 when asked.

## 10. See how an athlete develops ✅

> *"from last time they have this, and now they have this and they see how they
> develop"* — 13:00

Immutable screening history per athlete, a date picker that replays any past
assessment across the whole clinical view, report-to-report progress deltas,
and the individual PDF's progress section.

## 11. Per-sport prominent problems, to advise on ✅ *(closed 2026-08-09)*

> *"the doctor in the room can also see, okay, this spot, what are the
> prominent kind of injury and when going to happen? So you can also give them
> a good advice"* — 13:00

The **coach** has this: squad focus, muscle hotspots and readiness by event for
their sport. The **medical** view lost its sport-context card when the injury
data went — rebuilt 2026-08-09 from screening instead, as a **Sport context**
card on the athlete detail (`GET /athletes/:id/sport-context`). It names what
the squad carries most and where this athlete stands against it, e.g.
*"Badminton carries Knee most: 3 of 15 elevated (20%). This athlete is furthest
above their squad on Ankle (+15.5 vs the squad average)."* Indicators are
ordered by what is prominent in the SPORT, not by the athlete's worst reading,
because the quote asks what the squad tends to have.

## 12. Two audiences, two different dashboards ✅

> *"one is administrator … we need to show them like summary of how the whole
> program perform"* … *"Another group is for professional, which is medical
> doctor, physio, or strength condition … They want to see individual and their
> trend of individual"* — 13:55–14:28

Exactly the split that shipped. `/admin/dashboard` is population-level;
`/medical/dashboard` is athlete-first with the cohort as context. FYP II added
a third shape — the sport-scoped coach board — which was not requested but sits
naturally in the "professional" group Dr Thung described.

## 13. Athlete data must stay confidential ✅ (with a caveat)

> *"the data includes some personal data from the athletes … It is not
> shareable to anybody else"* — 19:47
> *"whether you are in ISN or not. Still, we need you to keep it to yourself and
> make sure data is safe all the time"* — 21:51

The strongest answer in the system: the athlete's name is located by local OCR
and blacked out **before any page image is transmitted** to the vision model,
failing closed if it cannot be located. The identity-to-assessment link is
re-established locally against ISN's own roster, so it is never transmitted
either.

⚠️ Caveat to hold alongside it: the same system now uses the IC number as its
cross-table key, so a national identifier appears in URLs, PDF filenames and
logs. §9 gives the reason; the honest framing is that identifiers are minimised
*leaving* the institution and increased *within* it.

## 15. Deployment on ISN's own server ⏸ not done

> *"maybe they can't really use public server. They might need to use the local
> server within their ISM server"* — 29:30 (Dr Hoo, in Dr Thung's hearing)

AIRMS runs locally in development and is not deployed to ISN infrastructure.
The architecture does not obstruct it: MySQL is self-hosted, and the vision
provider is deliberately provider-agnostic including a **fully local Ollama**
option, so athlete images need never leave the premises. Deployment is an
operational gate (Dr Thung's sign-off), not a code gap.

## 16. Standard, automatic, monthly reporting ⚠️ partial

> Dr Hoo: *"maybe you want to think about reporting, automatic reporting as one
> of the features … given feature that they actually selected we can do some
> pdf"* — 30:26
> Dr Thung: *"I think something standard, then you can actually generate every
> month."* — 31:16

Three standard PDFs exist (holistic, individual, team), they honour the
selected filters, and the holistic one now takes a period grain — so the
*content* of a monthly report is there and reproducible. What is missing is the
**automatic** part: nothing generates or emails it on a schedule; an
administrator downloads it. Given the alert mailer already exists, scheduling is
a genuinely small addition if he wants it.
