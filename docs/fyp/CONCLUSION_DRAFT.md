# Chapter 7: Conclusion (Draft)

The Athlete Injury Risk Management System (AIRMS) represents a comprehensive and integrated solution to the challenges faced by institutional sports health management at Institut Sukan Negara (ISN). Conventional approaches are often characterized by fragmented spreadsheets, manual injury records, the absence of workload monitoring mechanisms, and the lack of structured analytics for administrative reporting. These limitations hinder injury prevention efforts, delay clinical decision-making, and restrict ISN's ability to identify systemic risk patterns across its 52 sports programmes. The AIRMS platform was developed to address these gaps by centralizing athlete activity tracking, injury surveillance, and population-level analytics within a single role-based digital ecosystem.

At the core of the system, the Activity Tracking and Workload Module enables athletes to log their training sessions and automatically derives an internal load value using the session-RPE method established by Foster et al. (2001). This load is aggregated across rolling weekly windows to compute the Acute-to-Chronic Workload Ratio (ACWR), which is then translated into a Low, Moderate, or High risk classification consistent with the thresholds validated by Andrade et al. (2020). By presenting workload data through intuitive visual indicators on the athlete dashboard, the system equips both athletes and medical staff with timely, data-driven information for identifying dangerous workload patterns before injuries occur.

The Injury and Recovery Logging Module formalizes a multi-stakeholder injury surveillance workflow consistent with the architecture identified as most effective by Costello et al. (2024). Medical staff record official injury entries with full clinical fields, while athletes are provided with a dedicated self-report pathway whose submissions flow into a Pending, Approved, or Rejected review state managed by medical staff. This separation preserves clinical accountability while capturing injury observations that may otherwise go unreported, addressing the persistent underreporting bias identified in the surveillance literature.

In addition, the Admin Injury Analytics Dashboard transforms longitudinal injury records into administrative intelligence. Grounded in the IOC consensus data variables defined by Bahr et al. (2020), the dashboard supports filtering by sport, gender, age group, body part, injury type, and date range, and visualises temporal trends across monthly, quarterly, and yearly periods. A live PDF report generation facility further enables administrators to produce documentation consistent with institutional reporting requirements, addressing the manual and unreliable nature of current administrative summarisation processes.

A complementary contribution is the Medical Staff Dashboard, which provides individual-focused traceability through athlete search and consolidated profile views. Medical personnel can review an athlete's full injury history, workload trend, and sport-level context in a single view, supporting longitudinal clinical decision-making across the breadth of ISN's elite, development, and competition-based programmes.

From a technological perspective, AIRMS demonstrates a robust and scalable architecture built on Next.js 14, Node.js with Express, and MongoDB Atlas. The implementation of JWT-based authentication and role-based access control ensures secure, tailored functionality for athletes, medical staff, and administrators, while the schema-validated data model preserves the longitudinal integrity required for credible workload and injury analytics.

In conclusion, the project objectives established for this academic phase were successfully addressed. The first objective was fulfilled through formal collaboration with ISN, beginning with the signed collaboration letter and the initial stakeholder meeting with Dr. Thung, which informed both the functional requirements and the data model. The second objective was accomplished through the design and development of all six functional modules, integrating activity tracking, workload computation, injury surveillance, data import, and role-specific dashboards into a single deployable platform. The third objective will be evaluated more comprehensively in the subsequent academic phase through formal User Acceptance Testing with ISN personnel. Overall, AIRMS effectively bridges critical gaps in existing institutional sports health management and establishes a strong foundation for full deployment, contributing toward a more proactive, evidence-based, and clinically credible approach to athlete welfare at ISN.
